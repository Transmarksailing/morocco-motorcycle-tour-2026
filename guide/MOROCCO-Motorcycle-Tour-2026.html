🗺️ The tour at a glance

Moraira → Almería (ferry) → Nador → Guercif → Midelt → Ifrane/Azrou → Merzouga (Sahara) → Todra & Dades → Aït Benhaddou → Marrakech → Ouzoud → Beni Mellal → Midelt → Nador (ferry) → Almería → Moraira

A grand loop from Moraira: a short run down the coast to Almería, the night ferry to Nador, then ten days through the very best of Morocco. From the green Middle Atlas with its cedar forests and Barbary macaques, down the Ziz Valley into the Sahara and the dunes of Erg Chebbi, through the spectacular Todra and Dades gorges, past the UNESCO kasbah of Aït Benhaddou, over the legendary Tizi n'Tichka pass to Marrakech, and back north via the Ouzoud waterfalls to the port.

Why October? The ideal month for Morocco: daytime 20–30 °C, comfortable desert temperatures, crisp mornings in the Atlas, little rain, clear skies and fewer tourists than in summer.

🏍️ Our four bikes — group notes

We're riding two BMW R1200RS, one Honda GB350 and one Yamaha Ténéré 700 World Raid. The route is deliberately planned on tarmac: the R1200RS is a road-focused sports-tourer, the GB350 a light, relaxed classic, and the World Raid, with its large tank, is built for long distances. Every stage is ridden entirely on tarmac; the odd gravel section is optional and only for the World Raid.

  • 🐢 Pace — the GB350 sets the rhythm. The Honda GB350 (± 21 bhp) is the most relaxed of the group. On the long days (day 10: 335 km) and the high passes (Tizi n'Tichka, 2,260 m) it's the slowest → allow a little extra time and stops, and don't ride away from the slowest bike. This is a savour-it route, not a race.
  • ⛽ Fuel range & group stops. The Ténéré 700 World Raid (23 L, ± 400 km) and the economical GB350 (± 15 L, ± 350 km) have ample range. The two R1200RS (± 18 L) realistically manage ± 300 km and therefore set the fuel stops: refuel together at ± 220–250 km, and never skip a station before a desert or mountain stage (days 4, 5 and 10).
  • 👥 Riding in formation. Ride in a staggered formation, with the slowest/least experienced rider at or near the front so nobody has to chase, and the last rider (sweep) keeps everyone together. Agree hand signals and a fixed regroup point (e.g. every junction/fuel stop).
  • 🛣️ Tarmac & luggage. The two R1200RS and the Honda GB350 are not made for loose gravel or pistes — so the whole group stays on the sealed road. The optional gravel sections (e.g. Cirque de Jaffar) are at most for the World Raid rider, and only in dry weather. Panniers/side cases are perfectly fine for this touring route; no soft sand.
  • 📄 Documents × 4. Each bike/rider needs their own set of papers (see border checklist). Green card + temporary Moroccan vehicle import per vehicle.
  • 🔧 One shared set of tools + spares is fine, but per bike carry: puncture-repair kit/pump, brake pads, chain lube (World Raid), and a set of fuses. Tyre sizes and oil differ per make.

🚢 The crossing — Almería ↔ Nador

The ferry takes ± 6–8 hours across the Mediterranean. Ideally take a night boat with a cabin in both directions, so you don't lose a riding day and arrive in Nador fresh in the morning.

  • Booking: Baleària or Trasmediterranea/Naviera Armas — choose Almería → Nador (not Melilla; Nador is in Morocco itself, while Melilla is Spanish and involves an extra border crossing).
  • Bike + cabin: book an outside cabin and state that you're travelling with motorcycles (lashed down on the vehicle deck).
  • Arrival Nador: port of Beni Ansar. Allow 1–2 hours for border formalities (passport stamp + temporary vehicle import).
  • 🛳️ Timetable & overview — Directferries

💡 Tip: book the return (day 10, Nador → Almería) with some margin; arrive at the terminal at least 3 hours before departure.

🏍️ Day 2 — Monday 12 October: Guercif → Midelt (N15)

Route: Guercif → N15 → Midelt · Riding time: ± 4 h · Distance: 238 km · Road surface: ★★★★★ · Scenery: ★★★★☆

  • 🛣️ The N15 — "quiet, flowing roads, perfect for a GS". One of the favourite riding roads of the whole trip: little traffic, long flowing bends and a gradually climbing mountain landscape towards the High Plateaus.

  • 🏨 Overnight — Kasbah Asmaa, Midelt. Characterful kasbah style with a palm garden and pool; an ideal base (we sleep here 3 nights in total: days 2, 3 and 9).

Route: Midelt → Ifrane → Azrou (cedar forest) → Midelt · Riding time: ± 4.5 h · Distance: 285 km (loop) · Scenery: ★★★★★

A day without luggage — we leave the bags in Midelt and ride a beautiful loop through the greenest part of Morocco.

  • 🌲 Cedar forest near Azrou · Vast cedar and pine forests; a good chance of wild Barbary macaques by the roadside.
    📍 Cèdre Gouraud / cedar forest · 📷 Photos

    📍 Azrou

💡 The loop can be shortened; anyone who prefers a rest can explore Midelt and its surroundings (the Cirque de Jaffar piste is for the World Raid only — gravel, dry weather; the two RS stay on tarmac).

🏍️ Day 4 — Wednesday 14 October: Midelt → Merzouga (Ziz Valley & the Sahara)

Route: Midelt → Errachidia → Ziz Valley (N13) → Erfoud → Merzouga · Riding time: ± 4.5 h · Distance: 285 km · Scenery: ★★★★★

  • 🏞️ Ziz Valley (N13) · The great transition from mountains to desert: a green ribbon of tens of thousands of date palms between red-brown rock walls. Stop at the Ziz viewpoint.
    📍 Ziz Valley viewpoint · 📷 Photos
  • 🐪 Arrival Erg Chebbi / Merzouga · The first real sand dunes (up to ± 150 m high). Ride the final stretch towards the foot of the dunes.
    📍 Merzouga / Erg Chebbi · 📷 Photos

🛃 Border & documents checklist (per bike × 4)

Make sure each rider carries these papers, complete and with copies:

  • ✅ Passport (still valid ≥ 6 months)
  • ✅ Ferry booking confirmation (outbound + return)
  • ✅ Vehicle registration document (V5C logbook) — in your own name, or a signed authorisation letter if not
  • ✅ Driving licence (UK photocard; an International Driving Permit is recommended)
  • ✅ Insurance certificate / green card with Morocco cover (check that Morocco is listed on your green card; if not, take out cover at the border on arrival)
  • ✅ GB registration / UK number plate clearly displayed (a "UK" sticker if your plate doesn't show it)
  • ✅ Temporary vehicle import (D16 / Déclaration) — filled in on entry; keep it safe, you hand it back on departure
  • ✅ Copies of everything (on paper + a photo on your phone)

⚠️ Important: on entry you receive a temporary import document per vehicle. Don't lose it — failing to hand it back on departure causes problems. Don't overstay the permitted period unintentionally.

🧳 Packing list — Morocco motorcycle trip (October)

Clothing & protection

  • Motorcycle jacket + trousers with removable thermal lining (Atlas mornings are cold, the desert is hot)
  • Waterproofs (low chance, but the Atlas can turn)
  • Gloves: summer + mid-season
  • Buff/neck tube against dust, sunglasses
  • Light clothing for the evenings

Bike & technical

  • Puncture-repair kit + mini pump/CO₂ per bike
  • Basic tools, cable ties, tape, duct tape
  • Spares: brake pads, fuses, chain lube (World Raid), bulb/LED
  • Charging cables, power bank, phone mount, spare GPS cable if you have one
  • Tie-downs (2 light straps for the ferry vehicle deck)

Personal

  • First-aid kit, high-factor sun cream, anti-diarrhoea tablets, rehydration salts
  • Cash (euros to change) + card; not everywhere takes cards
  • Local SIM or eSIM
  • Copies of documents, small change for tea/parking attendants

🇲🇦 Mini Morocco handbook for the road

  • Checkpoints: police/gendarmerie checks are normal. Stop calmly, greet, show your papers, smile. Usually you're waved through.
  • Speed: in built-up areas often 40–60 km/h; speed cameras occur. Stick to the signs.
  • Fuel: plenty in the north/cities; before desert and mountain stages (days 4, 5, 10) always fill up.
  • Water & food: drink bottled water; tagine, couscous, brochettes and fresh mint tea are safe and delicious everywhere.
  • Money: the dirham (MAD) can't be traded freely outside Morocco — change/withdraw locally and keep some cash for small villages.
  • Language: Arabic + Berber; French is the second language. A few words of French/Arabic open doors.
  • Tips & guides: a small tip for parking attendants (gardien) is customary (5–10 MAD).
  • Emergency numbers: police 19, gendarmerie (outside towns) 177, ambulance 15.

📲 Loading the GPX into your Garmin Zümo XT / Kurviger

Best for Kurviger, Calimoto, TomTom etc.: use the per-day files in the gpx-per-dag/ folder — one clean route per stage (5–8 via points), which import without the "too many markers" warning. The single combined file reisgids/Marokko_Motorreis_2026.gpx is best for the Zümo XT (it also contains all the POI waypoints).

  1. Connect the Zümo XT via USB (or use the Garmin Tread / Drive app) and copy the file to the Garmin/GPX folder.
  2. On the Zümo: Apps → Trip Planner / Routes → import.
  3. ⚙️ In Settings → Navigation → Avoidances, tick Motorways and Toll roads for the most scenic roads.
  4. For Kurviger: open a per-day file via Import GPX; it loads as a route with a handful of waypoints you can drag to fine-tune.

💡 Tip: also works in Komoot, OsmAnd, Apple Maps and Google My Maps. In remote areas, load offline maps as a backup.

⚠️ Good to know

  • Book early: Merzouga (dune lodges), Dades (Chez Pierre) and Marrakech (riads) fill up quickly in October. Book the ferry (outbound + return) early too.
  • Prices are indicative (Oct 2026) and not checked in real time — always confirm when booking.
  • Riding times exclude stops and assume scenic roads.
  • Don't lose the border document (temporary vehicle import, per bike).
  • Desert = no soft sand on laden touring bikes. Stay on firm ground; leave the dunes to guides/quads.
  • Return ferry: in good time, documents ready, photograph where the bike is parked.
