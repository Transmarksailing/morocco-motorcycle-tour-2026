# 🚑🔧 Morocco Motorcycle Tour 2026 — Emergency & Services

Hospitals, motorcycle/tyre garages and breakdown info along the route. **Save the emergency numbers in every phone before you go.**

> ⚠️ **Phone numbers marked *(verify)* should be reconfirmed on the day** — small-town Moroccan hospital/garage lines change and desert villages have thin online data. Emergency short-numbers below are reliable; when in doubt dial **112**. Moroccan format shown for domestic dialling; from abroad replace the leading 0 with **+212**.

## 📞 National emergency numbers (free · 24/7 · any phone)

| Number | Service |
|---|---|
| **112** | **Universal mobile emergency** — best default, works on any/foreign SIM |
| **150** | **SAMU / medical ambulance** (a few listings say 141) |
| **15** | **Protection Civile** — fire, road accidents, rescue |
| **19** | **Police** — inside towns/cities (sometimes shown as 190) |
| **177** | **Gendarmerie Royale** — rural roads, highways & desert → **your main number outside towns** |
| **5050** | **ADM** motorway (autoroute) assistance |

> 📶 In the Atlas and desert, mobile coverage is patchy — **don't assume you always have signal.** On rural roads **177** is the number that answers for accidents/breakdowns.

## 🛠️ Breakdown & recovery — how it really works

- **There is NO ANWB/AA-style national motorcycle breakdown service** in Morocco. Don't expect a phone-a-tow network.
- **On rural roads the Gendarmerie Royale (177) is the de-facto first responder** — they secure the scene and can summon a local tow (*dépanneuse*) or garage, but they don't repair.
- **Recovery is local & informal:** the nearest town's garage sends a flatbed/pickup — **agree a price up front** (negotiate). Small towns fix small bikes; big-bike expertise/parts are limited.
- **Your real safety net is your own insurance.** Carry your **green card** (extend the Morocco cover at the border/ferry) **and your insurer's international assistance number — written on paper, not only in your phone.** Note: ANWB does not run in-country moto recovery in Morocco; check your policy (e.g. ADAC and some international policies do cover Morocco).
- **Be self-sufficient:** tools, tubeless plug/repair kit, mini-compressor or CO₂, spare tube(s) (Ténéré), spare chain link, levers, zip-ties, tape. For a serious mechanical failure, plan to **truck the bike to Marrakech or Casablanca** where the real dealers are.

## 🛞 Tyres — the reality for R1250GS & Ténéré 700

- **Don't assume your size is available on the road.** Adventure/knobbly and premium touring sizes for big bikes (GS 19"/17", Ténéré 21"/18") are **scarce outside Marrakech and Casablanca**.
- **Best stock:** **Casablanca** (national) — **BMW Motorrad Maroc 0522 40 07 00** *(verify)* for the GS; **MIFA Motors / Yamaha 0522 04 06 50** and moto shops (ETS Samouh, Motomania) for the Ténéré. **Marrakech** = best in the south (MIFA/Yamaha + Smart Auto Moto). Call ahead to confirm your exact size.
- **Advice:** carry a proper **tubeless repair/plug kit + spare tube(s)**. If you run an unusual size or a specific compound, **bring a spare tyre** or pre-arrange one at a Casablanca/Marrakech dealer **before departure** — don't count on buying adventure rubber spontaneously mid-tour.

## 🧰 Documents to carry (each rider)
Passport · vehicle registration/logbook · driving licence (+ IDP) · **green card valid for Morocco** · **insurer assistance number** · temporary vehicle import (D16ter, from the border) · travel/health insurance details · blood group noted. Keep paper copies.
